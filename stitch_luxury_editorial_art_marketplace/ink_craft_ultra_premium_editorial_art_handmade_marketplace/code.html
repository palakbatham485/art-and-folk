Master In Residence • Kyoto, Japan

Mei Lin Takahashi

"Craft is the silent dialogue between human hands and organic matter."

Spanning over three decades of mastery, Takahashi’s creations bridge the tactile wabi-sabi ethos with modern architectural silhouettes. Utilizing clay sourced exclusively from the Shigaraki hills and fired in wood-fueled anagama kilns for 96 continuous hours, no two pieces emerge with identical mineral crystallization.

Her works have been permanently acquired by the National Museum of Modern Art Tokyo and private collections across Zurich, Seoul, and Manhattan.

Our Manifesto & Ethos

Every Creation Has a Story.

We reject mass-produced uniformity in favor of singular provenance, honoring centuries-old techniques refined by today's master practitioners.

I. The Sculptor’s Hand

Every contour shaped with manual hand tools and instinctive patience.

II. Earth & Element

Mineral-rich unrefined clays fired in wood kilns to capture nature's unpredictability.

III. Botanical Alchemy

Roots, madder, marigolds, and indigo leaf pigments fermented slowly without harsh chemicals.
